## Full Summary

Pre-registered model-screen evaluation comparing five cooling-model candidates (B0 Newton single-exponential, B1 two-exponential piecewise, C1 coupled temperature-mass ODE, C2 residual-structure change point, C3 flux-crossover switching with crema-coverage decay) on a panel of 3 independent published coffee cooling datasets (Manitoba calculus workshop, Kurzweg UFL 2017, Ferreira cup 2024). The evaluation applies the pre-registered survival rule verbatim: a candidate survives iff it has the lowest heldout OOS RMSE on >=80% of datasets AND mean delta-BIC > 10 over Newton baseline B0. Results: NO SURVIVORS. At N=3 datasets, the 80% rule degenerates to unanimity (3/3 required). C3 (flux-crossover) achieved the best mean heldout RMSE (0.275°C) and best crossing-time error (0.06 min) but has negative delta-BIC (-13.0), failing the BIC screen. C1 (coupled ODE) is the closest candidate with positive delta-BIC (18.3) and mean heldout RMSE 1.819°C. Paired 5000-draw bootstrap 95% CIs on RMSE differences confirm C3 is significantly better than C1 (mean diff -1.544, 95% CI [-2.842, -0.821]) but the BIC penalty for the extra parameter is decisive. C3 tau-ablation shows the MLE tau (8.0 min) is near-optimal on 2/3 datasets; the sweep provides only 0.06°C mean improvement. Power note: at N=3 the minimum detectable effect at 95% power is ~2.5°C, so the panel is underpowered for small effects. The honest null finding is: Newton's law is adequate at the drinkable crossing for these datasets given the BIC penalty for complexity, and the crema-breakdown hypothesis needs more datasets or a more parsimonious formulation to survive the screen.
